# CI-001: Search Intent Alignment

**Pillar:** Content Intelligence™
**ID:** CI-001
**Version:** 1.0.0
**Status:** Active

---

## Objective

Evaluate whether the primary content of each key page correctly matches the dominant search intent expressed by the queries for which that page is appearing or targeting — and whether the content type, format, and structure are consistent with what Google's ranking systems recognize as the correct response to that intent.

---

## Business Importance

Intent mismatch is the most common reason that pages with reasonable keyword targeting, adequate content depth, and functional technical setup fail to sustain rankings. When a user searches with informational intent and lands on a transactional page — or vice versa — the resulting engagement signals communicate to Google that the result was not satisfying. Over time, consistently poor engagement signals erode ranking position regardless of other optimization.

Resolving intent mismatch is often one of the highest-leverage changes available in a content audit because it requires restructuring or repositioning content rather than creating new content, and because it directly improves both search performance and conversion rate.

---

## Google Search Importance

Google's ranking systems are trained on the relationship between queries and user satisfaction. Google's documentation references "understanding the search intent behind a query" as a primary function of its systems. The Search Quality Rater Guidelines define several dimensions of query intent, and Google's Quality Raters explicitly evaluate whether search results satisfy the needs of users performing specific queries.

Google analyzes multiple signals to infer the dominant intent for a query, including:
- The content types that currently rank for the query (if listicles rank, the intent is likely informational)
- Historical click-through and engagement data for different result types
- The vocabulary and syntax of the query itself

---

## AI Search Importance

AI search products synthesize information to answer user questions. They are specifically optimized to match the intent of the question with the content of their sources. Content that mismatches intent at the page level will be deselected as a source even when it technically contains relevant information. A product page that technically mentions how a process works will not be selected to answer a "how does X work" question when dedicated informational pages from other sites are available.

---

## Applicable Website Types

- [x] All websites

Intent alignment is universally relevant. The complexity of the evaluation varies by site type: informational and content-heavy sites have simpler intent patterns; e-commerce sites have complex intent landscapes across category, product, and informational content.

## Prerequisites

- [ ] Search Console performance data available with at least 28 days of data

- [ ] Access to live Google Search from a neutral IP (not logged in, not on a personalized search environment)

---

## Step-by-Step Audit Procedure

**Step 1: Identify primary queries for each key page**

In Search Console, navigate to Performance > Pages. Sort by impressions (descending). For each of the top 50 pages, click through to see associated queries. Identify the query with the highest impressions — this is the primary query for that page. Record it.

**Step 2: Classify the intent of each primary query**

For each primary query, classify the dominant intent:

- **Informational:** The user wants to learn, understand, or discover something. Query often contains "how," "what," "why," "guide," "tutorial," or is a question.
- **Navigational:** The user wants to reach a specific website or page. Query is typically a brand name or brand + page type.
- **Commercial investigation:** The user is evaluating options before making a decision. Query often contains "best," "top," "review," "vs," "compare," or "alternatives."
- **Transactional:** The user is ready to act. Query often contains "buy," "price," "order," "hire," "download," or is a product/service name with purchase-oriented modifiers.

**Step 3: Verify intent by analyzing SERP composition**

For each primary query, perform a live Google search. Review the top 5–10 results. Note the dominant content types:

- Listicles or how-to guides → informational
- Product pages or e-commerce category pages → transactional
- Comparison or review pages → commercial investigation
- Brand homepages or login pages → navigational

The dominant type in the top results represents Google's interpretation of the intent. If the evaluated page is a different type than the dominant SERP content type, intent mismatch is present.

**Step 4: Compare page content type to SERP-validated intent**

For each page, record:
- The page's content type (guide, product page, category page, landing page, etc.)
- The intent-validated content type (from Step 3)
- Whether they match

**Step 5: Assess page structure for intent delivery**

For pages where the content type matches the intent, assess whether the page structure leads with the intent-satisfying content:

- Informational pages: Does the primary answer appear within the first screen? Or is there a long introduction that delays the answer?
- Commercial investigation pages: Does the comparison or evaluation begin above the fold, or does the page begin with a product pitch?
- Transactional pages: Is the conversion action (Add to Cart, Contact Us, Request Quote) visible above the fold, or buried?

**Step 6: Check for mixed intent pages**

Identify pages that are attempting to serve multiple intents simultaneously (e.g., a page that begins with an informational guide and then transitions to a product pitch). For high-competition queries, mixed-intent pages typically underperform relative to pages that are purely committed to one intent.

**Step 7: Evaluate high-volume, low-ranking pages**

From Search Console, identify pages with very high impressions but low average positions (position 8+). These are pages that Google is showing for the query but not ranking highly. Intent mismatch is a common cause of this pattern.

---

## Passing Criteria

A checkpoint passes (score: 3) when:

- For all top 20 pages by organic impressions: the page content type matches the dominant intent type observed in the SERP
- Pages leading with intent-satisfying content (informational answer, comparison, or conversion action visible without scrolling)
- No pages with informational content type ranking for transactional queries or vice versa among top pages

A checkpoint receives a partial score (score: 2) when:

- Intent mismatches identified on fewer than 20% of the top 20 pages by impressions
- Most pages lead with intent-satisfying content, but some require scrolling to reach it
- Mixed-intent pages present but only on secondary pages (not among top 10 by impressions)

---

## Failure Indicators

- Product pages ranking for queries where all top 5 results are informational guides
- Blog posts or guides ranking for queries where all top 5 results are product pages
- Pages where the conversion action is below the fold on mobile for transactional queries
- High-impression pages consistently ranking in positions 8–15 without improvement across 3+ months (suggesting content-type mismatch)
- Engagement rate in GA4 below 40% for major landing page categories (indicating users are not finding what they expected)

## Common Mistakes

**Classifying intent from the query alone without verifying the SERP:**
Query language is an imperfect intent indicator. "Best CRM for small business" might seem informational, but if all top results are listicles with affiliate links, the commercial investigation intent is dominant. Always verify intent by reviewing what actually ranks, not just what the query says.

**Treating all informational queries as a mismatch for detailed pages:**
Some informational queries have sufficient depth that a comprehensive guide (long-form, detailed) is the correct content type, not a short answer. Evaluate the SERP composition for the specific query rather than applying a blanket rule.

**Ignoring secondary intents:**
Many queries have a dominant intent and a secondary intent. A "best accounting software" query has commercial investigation as its dominant intent, but also an informational secondary intent (users want to understand what makes one option better than another). Pages that serve only the dominant intent without acknowledging the secondary intent may be outperformed by pages that serve both.

---

## Recommended Fix

**For intent-mismatched pages (wrong content type):**

There are three options, ordered by implementation complexity:

1. **Create a new page** with the correct content type for the query, and redirect or reconfigure the existing page for a different, appropriate query. This is the most effective solution for high-value queries.

2. **Rewrite the existing page** to change its content type. This is only appropriate when the existing URL has significant external link authority that should not be abandoned.

3. **Defer to the existing content and find a different query** that is appropriate for the existing content type. This is appropriate when the mismatched page is not commercially important.

**For pages with correct content type but poor intent delivery:**

Restructure the page to lead with the intent-satisfying content:
- Move the primary answer, comparison table, or conversion CTA above the fold
- Remove or shorten introductory content that delays the intent delivery
- Reorganize heading hierarchy so the first H2 addresses the primary query need

---

## Validation Process

After restructuring or creating new pages for intent alignment:

1. Use the URL Inspection tool to request indexing of updated pages
2. Monitor Search Console position data over 30–60 days for the primary query
3. Monitor GA4 engagement rate for the affected landing pages — intent-aligned pages should show higher engagement rates

## Expected Business Impact

Resolving intent mismatches on high-volume pages typically produces:
- Improved average ranking position for the primary query (as Google observes better engagement signals)
- Higher conversion rates from organic traffic (because users arrive on a page that serves their actual need)
- More stable rankings over time (intent-aligned pages are less susceptible to position fluctuation)

The magnitude of improvement is proportional to the traffic volume and commercial value of the affected pages.

## Notes

**Intent shift over time:** The dominant intent for a query can shift as search behavior or the market changes. A query that was informational in 2022 may now be commercial investigation in 2026. Re-evaluate intent for high-priority queries at least annually.

**Local intent:** Many queries have local intent (the user wants results near their location) that is not apparent from the query itself. "Coffee shop" has implicit local intent. For businesses with physical locations, verify whether target queries have local intent by testing in a geographically relevant search context.
