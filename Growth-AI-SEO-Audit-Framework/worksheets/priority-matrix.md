## Purpose

Use this matrix to assign implementation priority to all audit findings after scoring is complete. Priority is determined by the intersection of two dimensions: **business impact** and **implementation effort**.

This matrix ensures that the action plan delivered to the client reflects both the urgency of issues and the practical constraints of implementation.

---

## Priority Classification Framework

### Step 1: Classify Business Impact

For each finding, assess the potential improvement in business outcomes if the finding is resolved.

| Impact Level | Definition |
|-------------|------------|
| Critical Impact | Directly blocking organic traffic, causing active penalty, or preventing conversion completion. Resolution expected to produce measurable results within 30 days. |
| High Impact | Meaningfully limiting organic visibility, content rankings, or conversion rate. Resolution expected to produce measurable results within 60–90 days. |
| Medium Impact | Limiting optimization ceiling but not causing active harm. Resolution expected to improve performance over 3–6 months. |
| Low Impact | Best practice gap with minimal current impact. Resolution improves quality but has negligible near-term performance effect. |

### Step 2: Classify Implementation Effort

For each finding, assess the resources required to resolve it.

| Effort Level | Definition |
|-------------|------------|
| Quick Win | Resolvable by a single person in under 4 hours with no development dependency. |
| Low Effort | Requires 1–2 days of work. May require one engineering task or CMS access. |
| Medium Effort | Requires 3–10 days across one or two disciplines (development, content, analytics). |
| High Effort | Requires more than 10 days, multiple stakeholders, or significant architectural change. |

## Priority Assignment Grid

Apply the following grid to determine the recommended implementation priority for each finding:

| | Quick Win | Low Effort | Medium Effort | High Effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical Impact** | P1 — Immediate | P1 — Immediate | P1 — Urgent | P2 — Planned |
| **High Impact** | P1 — Immediate | P2 — Planned | P2 — Planned | P3 — Scheduled |
| **Medium Impact** | P2 — Planned | P2 — Planned | P3 — Scheduled | P4 — Backlog |
| **Low Impact** | P3 — Scheduled | P3 — Scheduled | P4 — Backlog | P4 — Backlog |

### Priority Definitions

**P1 — Immediate:** Address within 2 weeks. Assign a responsible owner immediately.

**P2 — Planned:** Address within 30–60 days. Include in the next planning cycle.

**P3 — Scheduled:** Address within 60–90 days. Include in the quarterly roadmap.

**P4 — Backlog:** Address when capacity allows. Review annually.

## Sequencing Considerations

Beyond priority, some findings must be addressed before others regardless of priority assignment.

### Mandatory Sequencing Rules

1. **Fix technical blockers before content or authority work.** A robots.txt blocking error or a sitewide noindex must be resolved before any other optimization produces results.

2. **Fix analytics before optimizing conversion.** Without accurate conversion tracking, it is impossible to measure whether conversion optimizations are working.

3. **Fix crawl errors before link building.** Acquiring links to URLs that return 404 errors provides no value.

4. **Resolve manual actions before any ranking improvement work.** Sites under manual action will not improve regardless of other optimizations.
